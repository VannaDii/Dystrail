# Pixel-art acceptance standard

User correction, September 14: the first park/co-op/print-yard/hearing atlas was too realistic. Its rendering is rejected, even though its setting mappings and browser checks worked.

The established road art (`dystrail-web/static/img/journey/open-heartland-prairie.png`) is the style reference. New environments should use large, intentional square pixel clusters; stepped silhouettes; simplified readable props; restrained color ramps; and the same warm ochre, green, blue, and dark teal relationships. Lighting must read in those discrete shapes. Avoid fine photographic texture, smooth material shading, painterly detail, and grain pretending to be pixels.

Judge the source artwork at its native size and inside the actual desktop and phone scene crops. `image-rendering: pixelated`, downsampling, or a pixel filter cannot rescue an illustration whose forms were designed realistically. Preserve the satire's required props while simplifying how they are drawn. Generated lettering remains blank; localized text belongs in editable layers.

The initial four-scene generation is retained in provenance as rejected. Replacement art needs a fresh rendered review. Apply the same standard to subsequent setting and supporting-character production; a passing functional test is not art approval.
